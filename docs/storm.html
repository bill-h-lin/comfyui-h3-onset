What this is

A storm crossing a landscape. A dust wall arrives over an empty desert. Lightning takes a tree. The sea detonates against black rock. A dam goes over in a wall of white water. The birds come up off the field at dusk. Five shots, no people, no dialogue.

Every sound you hear was generated by H3 in the same pass as the picture it belongs to. Nothing was recorded, licensed, layered on, or moved between shots. The only editing is cutting and level ramps: the five source shots span 14.4 dB in peak level (lift 0.181 ... bolt 0.951), so the closing shot is lifted 11.4 dB and the opening one trimmed 2.8 dB to give the film an arc rather than a cliff at the end.

What this film does not do, so you don't have to guess: these shots let H3 generate its own audio. I did not command the timing of any of these impacts. The technique for commanding when H3 makes a sound is real and measured, and the node and the carrier wavs for it are below, but this cut does not use it. I would rather tell you that than let the sync read as authored when it is the model's own.

What this model will and will not do, and how it cost me three shots

I rendered eight environmental shots with the same graph, the same sampler and the same prompt skeleton, varying only the subject. They split perfectly along one line:

What the prompt asked forShotsDid the event happen on screen?
a rigid structure fails glacier calving, tower collapse, avalanche 0 of 3. The object is intact in the last frame. Particulate rises in front of it.
fluid or light acts lightning strike, dam discharge, wave impact, lava fissure 4 of 4. A real before-state, a real event, a real after-state.

The glacier is the clearest case. Its face is unchanged between the first and last frame: same top edge, same crevasse pattern. What reads as calving is a spray plume rising in front of the base. Two of three independent reviewers called it a successful calving from a 666 px-per-cell contact sheet. One of them described a chunk of the face as missing. Shown the native-resolution crop instead, both returned the opposite verdict at high confidence. The third had already reached that answer at the low resolution, by tracking the cliff's skyline rather than the plume. So an occluding effect and a destroyed object are the same picture until you have either the pixels or the right endpoint. The right endpoint is cheaper.

The measurement trap, which cost me the best shot in the set. I ranked takes on whole-frame mean frame-to-frame luminance change. That statistic is diluted by any large static area, so it put the dam, a dry wall going to a full-width torrent, near the bottom at 1.56, and it ranked the wave 5th of 8. The wave has the cleanest dramatic structure of anything I rendered: a held calm, a rear, a detonation, a settle. It was left out of the first cut entirely because of that number. The meter is good for deciding where to look. It cannot decide what happened.

The model starts mid-event unless the prompt gives it something else to hold. Two shots here promised an entrance and delivered a state already in progress. The dust wall was already filling frame 0, the birds were already airborne. The fix was not a better seed, it was removing the event from the opening clause: describe an empty desert, or birds sitting on the ground, and only then name what arrives. Re-rendered on that pattern, 1 of 3 dust takes and 3 of 3 bird takes held a real before-state, and both shots in this cut now do what the synopsis says.

The audio field is a level control. But this film is not the evidence for it, and I am retracting the version of this claim that said it was. overall_soundscape is not decoration; H3 renders the loudness you describe. But the earlier text here claimed a 21 dB gap between the shots prompted "Quiet still air and nothing else" and the shots that were not, and that contrast does not exist. Read back out of ComfyUI's /history: every one of the 20 candidate renders carries the identical soundscape line, and their raw peaks still span 23.7 dB (0.065 ... 0.989). That is within-condition subject and seed variance, not a treatment effect, and there was no control arm.
⭐ The properly controlled version of the claim is real and it is on the THE LAST HEAT page: same shot, only the soundscape's word order changed. Peak 0.0115 → 0.196, a 17x move with the noise floor unchanged.

The technique in the repo

A transient written into the reference audio comes back in the render at the time you wrote it: seven ticks placed at 1000/1900/2548/3015/3350/3592/3766 ms returned +27.0 … +12.2 dB over a byte-identical carrier, 3 of 3 seeds on every one, while seven decoy times with nothing placed read −1.4 … +1.0 dB (-0.3 / +0.1 / +0.2 / -0.4 / +0.6 / +1.0 / -1.4).

🔴 The limit is position, not gap density. In a 124-frame render everything commanded at or before ~3.02 s reproduces; everything at or after ~3.35 s does not. A 243 ms gap fires at 3.00 s and a 242 ms gap fails at 3.59 s. Same gap, different position, opposite result. Put your beats in the first three seconds.
⚠️ Scope, because the paragraph above appears to contradict this one. That train does show 3350/3592/3766 ms firing: on a quiet room-tone scene with a small object. The bound was measured on 2 subjects x 3 seeds x 124 f and it is those subjects it holds for. A late beat is not impossible; it is unreliable, and how unreliable depends on the scene.

And a pre-registered test that failed: the picture does not follow the score. Visible agreement is bought by selection across takes and by the cut, not by the model choreographing the image.

Three honest notes on the .json. (1) It is one shot's API graph. All five shots run the identical 15-node graph and differ only in prompt and noise_seed, so this is the build, not a sample of it. (2) There are two graphs on this page on purpose: h3_storm_workflow.json is the film; h3_fuse_workflow.json is the commanded-transient graph, which adds LoadAudio → H3ZeroAudioPrefix to bind a carrier wav. The film does not use it, and neither the carrier wavs nor H3PlaceTransient appear anywhere in the film's graph. (3) A trap worth your time: --ref-image / --ref-audio left unset do not mean "no reference" in every driver. Some bind a default. Pass the explicit negative, or read the dumped graph, or you will condition a render you believed was unconditioned.

Every render in this film was submitted by an agent, and you can audit that

All five shots were submitted through the Comfy MCP, not typed into the UI. The claim is checkable rather than asserted: ComfyUI records the submission path server-side in extra_data.comfy_usage_source (comfy-cli/comfy-mcp), on 5 of 5 of this film's source renders. Note the field does not sit at the top level of a /history entry. It is at prompt[3], and reading it from the wrong place makes a true claim look false.

To prove the graph an agent submitted is the graph that ran, diff the file you submitted against what ComfyUI's own /history says it executed, at every class_type and every input field. Mine returns 0 differences; the same diff against all 373 other executed prompts on this box at the time it was measured (2026-08-31) returns a minimum of 8; and flipping a single seed returns exactly 1, so the instrument resolves one field.

My first version of this gate was worthless and I threw it out. It compared outputs. ComfyUI's node cache made two submissions of one graph return the same artefact, so it passed by construction. A gate that cannot fail is not a gate. The /history comparison above replaced it because it can fail, and the two control numbers (min 8, seed-flip 1) are there to show it does.

One more thing I got wrong, because it is the useful one. To pick between seeds I scored takes on how much of the frame changed. For destruction shots that metric is unusable: a structure being destroyed and a dust cloud drifting in front of an intact structure produce the same number. It scored a tower take at 63.85 in which the tower never comes down, 4th of the 8 rows overall, because dust filled the frame. It also ran the other way, preferring a dam overtopping (75.62) to the dam breach in this film (47.20), and ranking the lightning strike, the best shot here, last of three at 7.45, because the tree is still standing at the end, just stripped. If you are choosing takes automatically for anything that explodes, floods, burns or collapses: occlusion and destruction are the same number. Score whether the silhouette changed, or look at the frames.

AI generation disclosure. Every shot in this film, picture and audio together, was generated by MiniMax H3 (reference-to-video) in ComfyUI v0.32.0, running locally on one RTX PRO 6000. No sound was recorded, licensed or overlaid; the audio in each shot came out of the same H3 pass as its picture. Editing was limited to cutting, level ramps at head and tail, and 120 ms of room tone taken from the two shots being joined. No people appear in the film.
