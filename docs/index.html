What this is

A glassworks is closing at six. The old master's hands can't turn another piece, so he puts his apprentice on the pipe and talks her through the last one — the gather out of the furnace mouth, her breath down it, the shaping on the bench, the pitch of the glass going thin. What comes out of it is a carafe. Then the furnace stops, and he names it: “first silence in forty years.” At dawn the aisle from the opening shot is cold and daylit, and there are two people standing in it instead of one.

Every sound you hear was generated by MiniMax H3 in the same pass as the picture it belongs to — the voices, the furnace, the ring of the finished glass. Nothing was recorded, licensed, layered on, or moved between shots, and there is no music. The whole edit is: a trim per shot, one gain per shot, a ±120 ms room-tone bridge at each join, and one limiter across the finished film.

About that bridge, stated plainly because it is the only thing added to the track. Every H3 render begins and ends at digital silence, so butting two shots together leaves a hole at the seam: measured across this film's 17 joins, the seam sat a median 69.7 dB below the louder of the two neighbouring beds, and at the worst join — audible room at −15.7 dB on both sides — the hole was 89 dB deep. A crossfade makes that worse, because both sides are already fading to zero. The fix has to add room tone. So each seam is filled with ±120 ms of room tone taken from the two shots being joined — nothing external, no third shot — which the organisers were asked about directly and cleared (“that’s totally fine… the 120 ms of room tone came from the original audio”). It lifts the median seam by 24 dB and the worst by 60 dB. It changes nothing more than 120 ms from a cut: 2.5 % of the samples in the film, all of them inside a join window, and the earliest speech in any shot starts at 0.50 s, so nothing is masked.

Four things this film measured about H3's audio, and one of them changed the script

These are two-render experiments anyone with the model can repeat. All four are measured on this box at 8 steps / 124 frames / 1344×768; n is small and stated per finding.

⭐⭐ 1. A shot carries a voice or a room. Never both. Adding a spoken line to an otherwise identical prompt collapses the shot's noise floor. Two matched pairs — same picture prompt, same requested ambience, the line is the only difference:

picturespoken line?peaknoise floor (10th pct)
furnace mouthno0.472−9.6 dB
furnace mouthyes0.799−57.9 dB
workshop wideno0.506−8.3 dB
workshop wideyes0.853−64.5 dB

48 dB and 56 dB. Across the whole batch the separation is complete with no overlap: 10 shots with a line, −49.6 … −65.9 dB; 4 silent shots, −8.3 … −35.1 dB. A prompt that asks in as many words for “a low continuous roaring furnace filling a large brick room” and a line comes back with the line and no roar at all.

⇒ This is a structural constraint on how you write a dialogue film with this model, and it cost me a shot. The ending was going to be a roaring furnace that dies away while the master names the silence — one shot, both things. It cannot exist: ask for both and you get neither. The fix is to split it across a cut — the room lives in a silent shot, the line lives in the next one, and the change lands on the edit. Which is where a film puts it anyway.

⭐ 2. overall_soundscape: is a level control, and its FIRST clause sets the level. Same shot, same seed family, same sentence, re-ordered:

soundscape linestereo peaknoise floor (10th pct)
“Near silence in a large empty room, then one single clear ringing chime of glass…”0.0115−65.7 dB
“One single clear bell-like ring of struck glass, loud and bright and close… decaying slowly through a large empty brick room.”0.196−66.4 dB

17× in peak from moving one clause — and the noise floor did not move at all. Asking for a quiet room first makes the whole clip quiet — including the event you asked for. Practical rule: name the loudest thing first, and never describe a sound by describing its absence.
🔴 And I walked into it again after writing that down. Both of the dawn prompts opened by naming a quiet room. Rewritten loudest-first with the picture clause held byte-identical and the seed unchanged: 5 of 6 paired takes came up (+7.9, +9.5, +5.6, +5.2, +3.9 dB; one regressed by 2.1). ⚠️ Honest cost: this is not “the same video with different audio” — changing the soundscape changes the prompt, so the picture re-samples too at a fixed seed. Neither re-render was adopted (pre-registered bar: louder and not a worse picture). The coda ships quiet, deliberately, with the alternative measured.

⭐ 3. A level fall within a shot is not promptable — the picture obeys and the audio does not. One prompt asked for a furnace “that sinks and dies away into near total silence” over a picture of the fire going out. The same sentence commanded both channels:

channelf0f30f60f90f120
picture (mean luma)24.321.914.28.75.6 ✅
audio (peak dB by fifth)−6.5−7.5−6.9−7.3−7.3 🔴

Only the picture obeyed. The model chooses the envelope; the edit chooses the dynamics. Put every level change on a cut.

⭐ 4. H3 will not render an unvoiced human breath. 5/5. Three independently worded prompts, two shots, two seeds, the breath named as the first clause: 88–97 % of the energy comes back below 200 Hz and 0.8–9.3 % in the 1–8 kHz band where a breath actually lives. What you get is a low rumble. The name-it-first fix that recovers a ring 17× does not recover the breath.
⛔ Scope, and it is the honest half: this is about an unvoiced breath as the foreground sound. Voiced speech renders well — 10 of 10 lines here were delivered with no invented head words. A struck-glass ring renders superbly. And this film contains the failure: shot 6 shows the apprentice blow down the pipe, and you can see it and you cannot hear it. I am not going to tell you the sound carries that moment. It doesn't. The picture does.

🔴 A trap worth your time. --ref-image / --ref-audio left unset do not mean “no reference” in every driver — some bind a default. Every render in this film was submitted by an agent, and you can audit that

All 18 shots were submitted through the Comfy MCP — not typed into the UI — and the cut is built from those MCP-submitted renders. The claim is checkable rather than asserted: ComfyUI records the submission path server-side in extra_data.comfy_usage_source (comfy-cli/comfy-mcp), on 18 of 18 of this film's source renders. Note the field does not sit at the top level of a /history entry — it is at prompt[3], and reading it from the wrong place returns None for every entry and makes a true claim look false.

To prove the graph an agent submitted is the graph that ran, diff the file you submitted against what ComfyUI's own /history says it executed, at every class_type and every input field. Re-derived on this film today:

comparisonfield-level differences
each published graph vs. its own executed prompt0, on 18 of 18 shots
the same graph vs. the nearest other shot's executed prompt (negative control)2 minimum, 2–3 across all 18
one graph vs. itself with a single seed changed (resolution control)1

So the instrument resolves a single field, and nothing else in 679 executed prompts on this box comes closer than two fields to any of these graphs. The margin is deliberately narrow: adjacent shots in one film share every loader, every sampler setting and the resolution, so the nearest confusable prompt differs only in prompt and filename_prefix. A wide margin here would mean the shots were not really the same build.

My first version of this gate was worthless and I threw it out. It compared outputs — and ComfyUI's node cache made two submissions of one graph return the same artefact, so it passed by construction. A gate that cannot fail is not a gate. The /history comparison above replaced it because it can fail, and the two control rows are there to show it does.

What the agent did NOT do, so you don't have to guess. Generation ran through the MCP end to end — author the graph, validate, submit, poll, fetch, screen, re-roll. Take selection, the measurement passes and the cut were not MCP, and one human judgement — whether the film sounds right — is not automatable and was not automated.

The node pack in this repo

H3ZeroAudioPrefix is not a demo — it is in 10 of this film's 18 graphs. H3 copies the first ~200 ms of the bound reference voice into the render, so a breath or a click at the head of your reference arrives in the render as a phantom sound before the first word. Measured: the reference's onset burst reproduced in 24 of 24 renders, Whisper transcribed a spurious word before the line in 16 of 24, and zeroing the reference's leading 200 ms removed it in 3 of 3. The node does that inside the graph, with a cosine ramp so the cut does not become a new click, and reports the before/after head peak so it shows its own evidence.

The second node, H3PlaceTransient, turns the same copying mechanism into a control surface: write transients into the reference audio and H3 puts sound events at those times. ⛔ THE LAST HEAT does not use it. No carrier wav and no placed transient appears in any of the 18 graphs — the furnace, the tools and the ring are H3's own timing. The technique, its measured limits and the pre-registered test of it that failed are documented in the repo README and the carrier files. I would rather say that than let this film's sync read as authored when it is the model's own.

AI generation disclosure. Every shot in this film — picture and audio together — was generated by MiniMax H3 (reference-to-video) in ComfyUI v0.32.0, running locally on one RTX PRO 6000. No sound was recorded, licensed or overlaid; the audio in each shot came out of the same H3 pass as its picture. Editing was limited to cutting, one level per shot, 50 ms fades at the joins and a final limiter. The two people in the film are synthetic and so is the chain that made them: both identity references are crops of frames from text-only H3 renders made on this box, so no photograph of any real person exists anywhere in the production. There is no recognisable brand mark, logo or lettering in any shot.
